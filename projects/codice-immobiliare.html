Codice Immobiliare

A rental-yield scanner for Rome. It scrapes sale listings from Immobiliare.it, collects 1,615 Booking.com calendars, joins both datasets spatially and compares purchase price per m² with short-let income per neighbourhood. The output is a ranked map of where the numbers look most interesting — and where they do not.

The question

Is the cheap zone actually the better investment?

Rome’s property market is usually discussed in €/m². For a short-let investor the more useful number is gross yield: how much annual rent a property can generate relative to its purchase price. That requires two datasets that do not share a key: sale listings and booking calendars.

The pipeline scrapes both, normalises prices and coordinates, filters to Rome, and joins listings to the nearest booking cluster. Zone-level rent and sale benchmarks are then used to compute yield and to flag listings priced above their own neighbourhood.

Headline: the median gross yield across the scanned zones is 4.99%. Peripheral zones such as Magliana, Portuense and Marconi screen higher on gross yield, while central zones trade at a premium that compresses it.
Method

Scan listings

Immobiliare.it crawler per zone, with price, size, floor, €/m² and the portal’s rent/sale benchmarks.

Scan the short-let supply

Booking.com property search plus calendar scraping: nightly rates, availability and review scores.

Join spatially

GeoPandas polygons and OSMnx road-network distances to associate each listing with its local market.

Rank & publish

Gross yield, over/under-pricing versus the zone, and an aggregated, non-identifying JSON payload.

Reading the numbers. Gross yield ignores taxes, fees, vacancy, maintenance, utilities and financing. Nightly rates are averages from a May 2025 snapshot and include both entire apartments and rooms. The purpose is screening and relative comparison, not an investment recommendation.
Engineering notes

What was hard

  • Scraping brittle portals

    Both sites change markup and use anti-bot measures. The collectors rely on explicit waits, retry paths and stable selectors rather than fixed XPaths.

  • Messy Italian numbers

    Prices arrive as “€ 269.000”, “€ 1.234,56” or plain floats. A single parser normalises thousands and decimal separators before any aggregation.

  • No shared key

    Listings and bookings are joined by geometry (zone polygons and nearest road-network nodes), not by address strings.

  • Privacy by design

    The published payload contains aggregates and anonymous coordinates only; raw scraped files stay out of the repository.
